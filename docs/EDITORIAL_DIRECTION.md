## 2. Editorial position

### 2.1 Core positioning

Position Faisal as a research-first academic whose public profile connects a coherent scholarly agenda to verifiable outputs, teaching, service, and responsible application. Research questions and evidence lead. Titles, affiliations, leadership roles, metrics, and chronology provide supporting context only when verified.

The editorial promise is:

> Help each visitor understand the verified research direction, inspect relevant evidence, and choose an appropriate next step.

This is an internal editorial promise, not homepage copy.

### 2.2 Positioning pillars and proof expectations

| Pillar | Editorial role | Minimum proof before public use | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coherent research direction | Explain the questions and themes that connect individual outputs | Approved research statement plus reconciled publications/projects mapped to each stated theme | Expertise, novelty, leadership, impact, or current activity from keywords alone |
| Verifiable scholarly record | Let publications and lawful source links substantiate the profile | Canonical, deduplicated metadata and working DOI/publisher links | Quality, influence, authorship contribution, or citation significance from venue/name alone |
| Responsible application | Show where verified work connects with systems, organisations, or public needs | Confirmed role, status/maturity, dates, partners, permissions, and outputs for each example | Deployment, adoption, benefit, funding attribution, partnership, or outcome without direct evidence |
| Teaching and academic contribution | Add evidence of teaching, supervision, and service without overwhelming the research story | Verified course titles, role records, consent-safe supervision data, and reconciled service evidence | Student outcomes, current availability, workload, duration, or totals not expressly documented |
| Accessible collaboration pathway | Give legitimate visitors a clear, bounded next step | Verified institutional email and approved inquiry categories | Guaranteed response, opportunity, funding, supervision place, speaking availability, or institutional commitment |

## 7. Readability and content density

These are editorial defaults, not rules for canonical titles, citations, legal text, or necessary technical strings.

- Use international English with British spelling as the house style: `catalogue`, `programme`, `organisation`, and `centre`. Preserve official names, publication titles, quotations, course titles, and source wording exactly.
- Aim for one main idea per sentence. Typical body sentences should be about 12–25 words; split sentences that exceed roughly 30 words unless precision requires the structure.
- Keep ordinary paragraphs to one–three sentences and usually 35–80 words. Use a short list when several parallel facts would otherwise create a dense paragraph.
- Page and section introductions should normally be one compact paragraph, no more than about 80 words.
- Headings should usually be three–ten words, concrete, and in sentence case. Do not use slogan fragments that hide the section purpose.
- Use direct verbs and concrete nouns. Prefer `The study evaluates...` to `An evaluation of... was undertaken` when both are accurate.
- Define a specialised term or acronym at first meaningful use for a mixed audience. Do not explain common interface terms or repeat definitions on every card.
- Keep citations, long publication titles, author lists, DOIs, and URLs intact and allow them to wrap. Do not shorten for visual neatness unless the full accessible value remains available.
- Use bullets for scannable parallel items, not for isolated sentences or to disguise an unclear hierarchy.
- Avoid exclamation marks, rhetorical questions as headings, all-capital prose, emoji, decorative symbols, and repeated em dashes.

Plain-language target: an educated international reader outside the immediate speciality should understand the page's purpose and the significance of each section without losing the terminology needed by expert readers. No numerical reading-grade claim is required.

## 8. House style conventions

### 8.1 Dates and time

- Use `19 August 2026` for full public dates; never `08/19/2026` or ambiguous numeric dates.
- Use `August 2026` when day precision is unnecessary and `2026` for year-only records.
- Use an en dash for a verified range: `2023–2025`. Use `Present` only when current status has been reverified for the release candidate.
- Distinguish `published online`, `issue date`, `appointment date`, `renewal date`, `observed on`, and `last verified`; do not collapse them into one date.
- Do not infer a public date from filename, file creation/modification metadata, upload date, or access date.
- For Islamic-calendar source dates, retain the source date internally and publish an approved English/Gregorian representation only after STR-004 defines conversion and citation rules.

### 8.2 Numbers, currencies, and metrics

- In ordinary prose, spell out zero through nine and use numerals for 10 and above. Use numerals consistently for dates, measurements, version numbers, money, percentages, citation data, and structured records.
- Use commas for thousands and a period for decimals. Preserve justified source precision; do not add or remove decimals to imply accuracy.
- Write Saudi riyals as `SAR [verified amount]`. State whether the amount is total award value, attributable amount, combined value, or another measure.
- Use `%` with a numeral in data-rich content. Explain the denominator when it is not obvious.
- Never merge totals from Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, ORCID, ResearchGate, a CV, certificates, or another source.
- Required metric pattern: `[value] [metric] — [source], observed [day month year]`.
- Avoid `more than`, `over`, `nearly`, or rounded shorthand when an exact approved figure is available; if rounding serves readers, label it and retain the exact source value in governed data.
- Metrics are optional. Omit a stale or contextless metric rather than decorating the profile with it.

### 8.3 Acronyms and technical terms

- Spell out a specialised term at first use, followed by its acronym in parentheses: `[verified full term] ([ACRONYM])`. Use the acronym alone only if it recurs meaningfully.
- Widely recognised audience terms such as `AI`, `CV`, `DOI`, and `PDF` may appear without expansion in interface labels; expand them in formal explanatory prose when clarity benefits.
- Preserve the official casing of ORCID, Scopus, Web of Science, Crossref, GitHub, and publisher/venue names.
- Define `zero-knowledge`, `federated learning`, `blockchain`, or other specialised concepts in plain language when the surrounding audience is broader than the speciality.
- Do not create an acronym for a theme, project, committee, or site feature merely to shorten copy.

### 8.4 Capitalisation, punctuation, and titles

- Use sentence case for page titles, headings, buttons, labels, filters, and table headings.
- Capitalise proper nouns and exact official names. Lowercase generic disciplines and roles in prose: `cybersecurity`, `artificial intelligence`, `assistant professor`, and `chief executive officer` unless they begin a sentence or form an approved displayed title.
- Use title case only for exact publication, course, project, programme, organisation, or formal role names when the canonical source does so.
- Use the serial comma in lists of three or more items.
- Use `and` in prose. Reserve `&` for an approved interface label such as the benchmark `Leadership & Service` or an exact proper name. STR-003 owns the final navigation label.
- Use double quotation marks for direct quotations and single quotation marks only within a quotation. Do not fabricate or lightly paraphrase a quotation.
- Use parentheses sparingly for source/status context. Prefer a separate metadata line when details accumulate.
- Use a colon to introduce a precise explanation or list; avoid repeated semicolons and em-dash-heavy promotional rhythm.

### 8.5 Hyphenation and compounds

Use these forms consistently when supported by approved content:

- `research-first`, `evidence-first`, `evidence-led`, `privacy-preserving`, `AI-enabled`, `zero-knowledge`, `real-world`, `cross-institutional`, `source-labelled`, `date-stamped`, `same-tab`, `full-text` as attributive compounds;
- `email`, `website`, `homepage`, `metadata`, `dataset`, `online`, `cybersecurity`, and `healthcare` as single words;
- `open access` as a noun and `open-access` as an adjective;
- `peer review` as a noun and `peer-review` as an adjective;
- `postgraduate` and `undergraduate` as single words; and
- `Leadership & Service` only if STR-003 retains the benchmark label; otherwise use `leadership and service` in prose.

Preserve canonical source spellings in publication titles, organisation names, and quotations even when they differ from house style.

## 9. Naming research, projects, publications, courses, and service

### 9.1 Research themes and directions

- Theme names are concise noun phrases in sentence case, normally two–six words.
- A theme must describe an evidenced body of work, not an aspirational market category.
- Label an insufficiently established topic `Emerging direction` only after CNT-003 verifies that public evidence or an approved current statement supports it.
- Do not use `expertise`, `specialism`, `leading`, `pioneering`, or `internationally recognised` as a theme label.
- Use the same approved theme name in navigation aids, filters, records, and related-content labels; do not create synonyms for variety.

### 9.2 Projects and research systems

- Preserve an official or author-approved project/system name exactly.
- When no approved proper name exists, use the canonical publication title or a transparent descriptive label marked internally as `[DESCRIPTIVE LABEL — VERIFY]`.
- Distinguish `funded project`, `research system`, `prototype`, `framework`, `study`, `dataset`, and `student project`; these terms are not interchangeable.
- Use controlled evidence-backed status language such as `concept`, `prototype`, `evaluated`, `available online`, `completed`, or `ongoing` only when the source supports the exact status and date.
- Never upgrade `proposed`, `conceptual`, `tested`, or `evaluated` to `deployed`, `operational`, `adopted`, or `impactful`.
- Name sponsors, partners, funding, and Faisal's role only after rights, attribution, amount, period, and public visibility are approved.

### 9.3 Publications

- Use the canonical publisher title, author order, venue, year/status, DOI, and article identifier after CNT-004 reconciliation. Do not apply house-style capitalisation inside a canonical title.
- Use `publication`, `journal article`, `conference paper`, `review`, or `preprint` according to the approved record type.
- Distinguish `published`, `available online`, `forthcoming`, and `preprint`; never use `published` as a universal status.
- Do not describe a publication as `high-impact`, `seminal`, `groundbreaking`, `top-tier`, `widely cited`, or `award-winning` without exact, current, context-appropriate evidence and editorial approval.
- `Featured` means editorially selected for relevance; it does not mean most cited, best, or institutionally endorsed. Record the selection rationale internally.
- Link labels name their destination: `Open DOI (external)`, `View publisher page (external)`, `View lawful full text (external)`, `View code (external)`, or `View data (external)`.

### 9.4 Courses, supervision, and student work

- Use the verified official English course title. Do not add course codes, sections, terms, rooms, delivery modes, student counts, or timetable details.
- An English translation of an Arabic course, role, committee, certificate, or project title is provisional until source reconciliation. Mark it internally `[TRANSLATION TO VERIFY: Arabic source; English candidate]`.
- Use `courses taught` only when the evidence supports teaching responsibility; do not substitute `courses`, `modules`, `curriculum`, or `expertise` without checking meaning.
- Use `supervised`, `co-supervised`, `mentored`, or `examined` only when the exact role is evidenced.
- Student names, project titles, outcomes, images, and quotations require the consent and privacy disposition defined by STR-004.

### 9.5 Leadership and service

- Use the evidence-backed formal role and organisation name; normalize Arabic/English title differences only through CNT-001/STR-004 rules.
- Separate `technology leadership`, `university service`, `peer review`, `community engagement`, and `speaking/professional development` rather than treating them as one impact claim.
- State contribution and period only to the precision the evidence supports. An undated decision yields no invented year.
- Do not infer chairing, leadership, responsibility, duration, outcomes, or current status from membership, a filename, a certificate, or inclusion in a document.
- Do not publish decision numbers, scans, signatures, QR codes, unrelated committee members, confidential manuscript information, or overlapping cumulative totals.

## 10. Claims, proof, and metrics language

### 10.1 Claim-strength ladder

Use the strongest level that the evidence supports—never the strongest wording available.

| Level | Permitted language | Required support |
|---|---|---|
| Source fact | `The publisher record lists...`; `[Role], [organisation], [verified period]` | Approved authoritative source for the exact field |
| Synthesis | `This work connects [verified themes]` | Multiple approved records plus an explicit, non-inflated editorial rationale |
| Status | `Available online`, `prototype`, `completed`, `ongoing` | Current source that defines the same status and date |
| Outcome | `Produced`, `evaluated`, `released`, `adopted`, `deployed` | Direct evidence of the exact output/outcome, scope, period, and attribution |
| Impact | `Improved`, `enabled`, `benefited`, `influenced` | Proportionate outcome evidence, baseline/comparator where relevant, and attribution review |
| Superlative/comparative | `first`, `leading`, `largest`, `top`, `best` | Independent, current, scoped evidence and explicit CNT-009 approval; otherwise prohibited |

Prefer a lower-level accurate statement to an ambitious unsupported one.

### 10.2 Metrics pattern

Every public metric, if approved, must include:

1. exact metric name and value;
2. source/platform;
3. observation date;
4. coverage or denominator note when the meaning is not self-evident;
5. canonical source URL when publicly appropriate; and
6. a freshness owner and expiry/review trigger.

Do not combine metrics across platforms, compare them without a justified method, call them `current` without a date, or repeat them across multiple pages from separate hard-coded sources. Never use a citation, publication, review, funding, course, student, or service total as an unsupported credibility badge.

## 12. Calls to action, links, and downloads

### 12.1 CTA taxonomy

| Intent | Preferred label | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explore a content area | `Explore research`, `View publications`, `View teaching`, `View leadership and service` | Primary/secondary internal navigation after STR-003 confirms labels | `Discover more`, `Learn more`, `Explore now` without a destination noun |
| Open a record | `View publication`, `View project`, `View research system` | Internal detail or expanded record only when that destination exists | `Read more`, `See details` repeated without context |

### 12.2 CTA hierarchy and mechanics

- Use one primary action per decision point and no more than two supporting actions unless the task requires otherwise.
- Contact and Download CV remain priority actions, but research evidence should precede them in editorial order.
- Labels use a specific verb plus destination/object. The same action uses the same label across surfaces unless context requires a more explicit accessible name.
- External links open in the same tab by default and include a visible or accessible external cue.
- A download label states file type and verified size; the adjacent metadata states the update date.
- Do not use an icon as the only label. Do not put the same URL into adjacent redundant controls.

## 13. Empty, error, loading, unavailable, and stale states

State copy follows a three-part pattern where applicable: **what happened; what remains true; what the visitor can do next**. It is calm, specific, and non-blaming.

| State | Editorial pattern | Illustrative microcopy—not final page copy |
|---|---|---|
| Loading | Name the content being prepared; avoid false progress | `Loading publications...` |
| Empty collection | State that no approved records are available; do not imply absence of work | `No records are available in this section yet.` |
| No search results | Name the filter outcome and offer recovery | `No publications match these filters. Clear filters or search by title.` |
| Data error | Name the failed content and a valid next step | `Publications could not be loaded. Try again or use the scholarly profile links.` |
| Link unavailable | Keep the record; explain that the destination is unavailable | `The external source is currently unavailable. The verified citation remains below.` |
| CV unavailable | Do not offer an old/private substitute | `The public CV is being reviewed and is not available for download.` |
| Email unavailable | Do not substitute private contact data | `The institutional email link is being verified. Please return later.` |
| Stale claim | Label the last verification and point to a current source or omit | `Last verified [day month year]. Check the linked source for current information.` |
| Image/font failure | Avoid copy about a missing decorative asset; retain meaning in text | Affiliation, content, links, and controls remain unchanged; no placeholder message is needed |
| 404 | State the missing page and offer task-relevant destinations | `This page could not be found. Explore research, view publications, or return to the homepage.` |

Do not use `Oops!`, `Something went wrong` without context, humor that trivialises failure, countdowns, blame, auto-disappearing error text, or unsupported service-restoration promises.

- Use the verified descriptor, page purpose, and one evidence/action cue. Do not keyword-stuff disciplines, roles, institutions, or methods.
- Never write `Official website`, `Official academic website`, `Taibah University faculty website`, or name Taibah University as publisher/owner.
- If affiliation appears in metadata, it is subordinate text and must be factually current. Do not add a university logo, symbol, template, or logo-derived social treatment.
- Do not include citation counts, publication totals, funding totals, `currently accepting`, or another volatile claim in metadata unless the source/date can be maintained everywhere it appears.
- Social copy may be slightly more concise than page prose, but it uses the same evidence, naming, endorsement, and availability rules. No hashtags, emoji, hype, or clickbait by default.

## 15. Prohibited phrasing and corrective examples

All examples below are fictional editorial demonstrations. Bracketed text is an authoring placeholder, not an approved factual claim or final page sentence.

| Problem | Do not write | Better editorial pattern | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional overclaim | `The official academic website of Dr. Faisal Albalwy at Taibah University` | `Faisal Albalwy — personal academic website` plus the separate verified affiliation line | Keeps personal ownership and affiliation distinct |
| Inflated expertise | `A world-leading cybersecurity and AI expert` | `[Verified academic title] and researcher focused on [verified research umbrella]` | Replaces a superlative with verifiable role/focus language |
| Prestige as evidence | `At prestigious Taibah University, he leads cutting-edge work` | `Institutional affiliation: Taibah University` plus separately evidenced research | Does not transfer institutional prestige or imply endorsement |
| Leadership-first profile | `[Executive title] and Assistant Professor transforming technology` | `Assistant Professor of Cybersecurity and researcher focused on trustworthy digital systems` **[all elements verify]** | Keeps research identity primary and removes impact rhetoric |
| Unsupported impact | `The system revolutionises secure healthcare` | `The study proposes [verified contribution] and evaluates it using [verified method].` | Names evidenced contribution and method, not assumed impact |
| Status inflation | `A deployed platform used across institutions` | `[Prototype/evaluated system/available implementation — use exact verified status]` | Prevents prototype-to-deployment escalation |
| Funding ambiguity | `He secured SAR [amount] in funding` | `Project funding: SAR [verified amount] — [source]; Faisal's verified role: [role].` | Separates award value, attribution, source, and role |
| Metric without context | `Over [number] citations` | `[value] citations — [platform], observed [day month year]; coverage varies by database.` | Makes source, date, and coverage explicit |
| Availability promise | `Currently accepting funded PhD students` | `For supervision inquiries, email [verified institutional email]. Availability and funding are not implied.` | Supports inquiry without inventing capacity or funding |
| Partnership overclaim | `Partner with Faisal and Taibah University` | `Discuss a research collaboration with Faisal` plus separate affiliation text | Avoids creating an institutional partnership claim |
| Institutional voice | `Our university research changes lives` | `My research examines [verified question].` or `The project investigates [verified question].` | Uses the personal/project subject rather than university `we` |
| Vague CTA | `Click here to learn more` | `View publications` or `Open DOI (external)` | Names the action and destination |
| Empty-state blame | `Your search returned nothing` | `No publications match these filters. Clear filters or search by title.` | States the system condition and recovery without blame |
| Stale current claim | `He is currently [role]` | `[Role], [organisation] — last verified [day month year]` | Makes currency observable |
| Translation inference | `Chair of [translated committee title]` | `[TRANSLATION TO VERIFY: source title; candidate English title]` | Prevents an unreviewed translation from becoming public copy |

Additional prohibited or controlled terms:

- Prohibited absent exceptional independent evidence: `world-leading`, `renowned`, `pre-eminent`, `pioneering`, `groundbreaking`, `revolutionary`, `best`, `top`, `first`, `largest`, `unmatched`, `visionary`, `transformative`.
- Controlled status terms requiring exact evidence: `current`, `active`, `ongoing`, `funded`, `operational`, `deployed`, `adopted`, `validated`, `successful`, `award-winning`, `certified`.
- Prohibited institutional relationship terms without exact authority: `official`, `endorsed by`, `approved by`, `in partnership with`, `on behalf of`, `powered by`, `university portal`, `institutional site`.
- Avoid generic promotional nouns/verbs: `innovation journey`, `thought leader`, `driving impact`, `shaping the future`, `unlocking potential`, `leveraging synergies`, `disrupting`, `redefining excellence`.

## 16. Authoring verification controls

### 16.1 Internal placeholder syntax

Content authors may use these exact internal markers while drafting. None may reach public output.

| Marker | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `[VERIFY: claim; preferred source(s); owner]` | A factual statement awaits evidence approval | `[VERIFY: current academic title; appointment record + current institutional profile; CNT-001]` |
| `[SOURCE: source ID or canonical URL]` | Attach an approved source to the adjacent claim | `[SOURCE: PUB-0001]` |
| `[AS OF: day month year]` | Date a volatile fact or metric | `[AS OF: 19 August 2026]` |
| `[OMIT IF UNRESOLVED]` | Enforce fail-closed publication behavior | `[VERIFY: supervision availability ...] [OMIT IF UNRESOLVED]` |
| `[TRANSLATION TO VERIFY: source language/title; English candidate]` | Flag a source-dependent English title | `[TRANSLATION TO VERIFY: Arabic committee title; candidate English title]` |
| `[DESCRIPTIVE LABEL — VERIFY]` | Distinguish a proposed label from an official name | `[DESCRIPTIVE LABEL — VERIFY] Privacy-aware system` |

### 16.2 Approval flow

1. CNT-001 registers the claim, source authority, conflicts, privacy class, and public disposition.
2. The responsible CNT writer uses this editorial policy to draft within the approved evidence boundary.
3. CNT-004 controls canonical publication wording and statuses; relevant content owners control other structured records.
4. CNT-008 checks cross-page UI labels, CTA consistency, footer/affiliation wording, and metadata descriptions.
5. CNT-009 independently confirms factual accuracy, English consistency, claim strength, naming, source/date labels, and zero unresolved authoring markers.
6. QA-002 repeats the claim/link audit on the frozen release candidate; QA-009 confirms the personal/institutional language boundary.

Release threshold: zero `[VERIFY]`, `[SOURCE]`, `[AS OF]`, `[OMIT IF UNRESOLVED]`, `[TRANSLATION TO VERIFY]`, or `[DESCRIPTIVE LABEL — VERIFY]` markers in public content or metadata. A resolved marker becomes governed content/data; an unresolved marker causes omission, not silent deletion of the uncertainty note.
